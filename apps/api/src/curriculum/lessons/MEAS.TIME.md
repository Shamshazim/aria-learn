## What it is

Working out what the clock will say after some minutes have passed, such as 2:45 and 30 minutes later.

## The one idea

An hour has sixty minutes, not a hundred. When the minutes reach sixty, the clock goes round and the hour moves on one.

## Common stumbles

- Adds the minutes like ordinary numbers and writes 2:75.
- Gets the minutes right but leaves the hour at 2, writing 2:15.
- Writes the 30 minutes as the new time, 2:30.

## Two concrete models

- A clock face, moving the minute hand round past twelve and watching the hour hand move.
- A number line of the clock from 2:45 to 3:15, jumping to the hour first.

## Worked example

It is 2:45. Fifteen minutes takes it to 3:00. That leaves fifteen more of the thirty, so 3:15.

## Language to use

- sixty minutes in an hour
- to the next hour, then the rest
- the hour hand moves on

## Language to avoid

- elapsed time interval
- base sixty
- modular arithmetic
