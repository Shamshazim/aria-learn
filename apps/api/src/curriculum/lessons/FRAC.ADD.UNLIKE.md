## What it is

Adding and taking away fractions whose pieces are different sizes, such as one half add one third.

## The one idea

Pieces of different sizes cannot be counted together. Cut both into pieces of one size first, then count.

## Common stumbles

- Adds the tops and the bottoms, and gets two fifths, which is less than the half it started with.
- Adds the tops and keeps the bigger bottom, getting two thirds.
- Changes both bottoms to sixths but leaves the tops, getting two sixths.

## Two concrete models

- Fraction bars: a half and a third laid end to end, then each re-cut into sixths.
- Two number lines, one in halves and one in thirds, matched up against a line in sixths.

## Worked example

One half add one third. Both two and three go into six. One half is three sixths. One third is two sixths. Three sixths and two sixths is five sixths.

## Language to use

- make the pieces the same size
- a bottom they both go into
- rename the fraction

## Language to avoid

- lowest common multiple
- least common denominator
- cross-multiplication
