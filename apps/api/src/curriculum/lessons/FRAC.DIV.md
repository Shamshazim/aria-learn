## What it is

Dividing a fraction by a fraction, such as three quarters divided by one eighth.

## The one idea

Dividing asks how many of the second fit into the first. Six eighths make three quarters, so the answer is six. The quick way is to keep the first fraction, flip the second, and multiply.

## Common stumbles

- Multiplies straight across without flipping, and gets three thirty-seconds.
- Flips the first fraction instead of the second.
- Flips both fractions.

## Two concrete models

- A bar of three quarters measured out in eighths, counting how many fit.
- Keep, change, flip written under the two fractions, one step at a time.

## Worked example

Three quarters divided by one eighth. Keep three quarters. Flip one eighth to eight over one. Multiply: three times eight is twenty-four, four times one is four. Twenty-four quarters is six.

## Language to use

- how many fit into
- keep, change, flip
- the one you divide by

## Language to avoid

- multiplicative inverse
- reciprocal of the divisor
- invert and multiply rule
