## What it is

Reading a written word of the form consonant, vowel, consonant by sounding out each letter and blending.

## The one idea

Each letter here makes one sound, left to right, and blending those sounds is the word.

## Common stumbles

- Guesses from the first letter and the picture instead of reading through the word.
- Reads the letter names rather than the sounds, saying see-ay-tee.
- Sounds out all three correctly but does not blend them into a word.

## Two concrete models

- A finger sliding under the word left to right, one sound per letter, then a fast sweep for the whole word.
- Letter cards pushed together as the sounds are blended, so the word arrives as the cards touch.

## Worked example

The word is cat. Point at c and say its sound. Point at a. Point at t. Now sweep under the whole word and say cat.

## Language to use

- sound it out
- what sound does it make
- sweep it together

## Language to avoid

- grapheme
- decode the string
- phonetic transcription
