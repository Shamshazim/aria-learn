## What it is

Dividing a three-digit number by a one-digit number one digit at a time, and saying what is left over.

## The one idea

Share the biggest place first. Whatever cannot be shared is broken into the next place down and shared again.

## Common stumbles

- Forgets the remainder, so one hundred fifty-seven divided by four becomes thirty-nine.
- Writes the remainder as a decimal, thirty-nine point one.
- Skips a place the divisor will not go into, so four hundred twelve divided by four becomes thirteen.

## Two concrete models

- Place-value blocks shared into equal groups, breaking a hundred into tens and a ten into ones when they will not share.
- The written method, one step per line: how many groups, take away, bring down.

## Worked example

One hundred fifty-seven divided by four. Four into one will not go, so take fifteen. Four into fifteen is three, which uses twelve. Three left. Bring down the seven: thirty-seven. Four into thirty-seven is nine, which uses thirty-six. One left. Thirty-nine remainder one.

## Language to use

- how many groups fit
- what is left
- bring down the next digit

## Language to avoid

- dividend and divisor
- the long division algorithm
- remainder theorem
