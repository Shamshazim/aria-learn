## What it is

Knowing the sums of two numbers that total ten or less quickly enough that the child does not have to count them out.

## The one idea

A fact you know frees your head for the problem. Numbers that make ten are the ones worth knowing first.

## Common stumbles

- Counts every addend from one instead of counting on from the larger number.
- Loses the count on fingers and lands one off, most often one short.
- Does not use the fact just learned when the same numbers appear the other way round.

## Two concrete models

- A ten-frame: put the first number in, and the empty squares show what is still needed to reach ten.
- A number line: start on the larger number and take the smaller number of steps.

## Worked example

Seven add three. Put seven counters in the ten-frame. Three squares are empty. Three fills it, so seven add three is ten.

## Language to use

- add
- makes ten
- count on from
- the same amount

## Language to avoid

- addend
- commutative
- sum of the operands
