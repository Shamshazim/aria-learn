## What it is

Finding a fraction that names the same amount with a different denominator, such as two thirds and eight twelfths.

## The one idea

Cut every piece into the same number of smaller pieces. The shaded amount does not change, so the top and the bottom are both multiplied by the same number.

## Common stumbles

- Adds instead of multiplying: twelve is three add nine, so the top becomes two add nine, eleven.
- Changes the bottom and leaves the top at two.
- Finds that three times four is twelve and answers four.

## Two concrete models

- Fraction bars: a bar in thirds above a bar of the same length in twelfths.
- A number line from zero to one marked in thirds and in twelfths, with two thirds and eight twelfths on the same point.

## Worked example

Two thirds is how many twelfths? Three times four is twelve, so each third is cut into four. Two thirds becomes two times four, eight twelfths.

## Language to use

- the same amount
- cut each piece into
- times the top and the bottom by the same number

## Language to avoid

- equivalent ratio
- scale factor
- cross-multiply
