## What it is

Seeing a fraction as a whole cut into equal pieces, and naming how many of those pieces you have.

## The one idea

The pieces have to be the same size. If they are not equal, the fraction name does not mean anything.

## Common stumbles

- Calls any four pieces quarters even when the pieces are different sizes.
- Reads the bottom number as how many pieces are shaded rather than how many the whole was cut into.
- Changes the whole partway through, comparing a piece of a small circle with a piece of a big one.

## Two concrete models

- A fraction bar: one strip cut into equal parts, shaded to show how many of them are being talked about.
- An array of equal squares: the same whole shown as a grid, so equal pieces are countable.

## Worked example

Cut a strip into four equal parts and shade one. The whole is four parts, so one part is one quarter. Cut a second strip into eight equal parts and shade two; two eighths shades the same amount of strip.

## Language to use

- equal pieces
- the whole
- one part out of

## Language to avoid

- numerator
- denominator
- partition the unit
