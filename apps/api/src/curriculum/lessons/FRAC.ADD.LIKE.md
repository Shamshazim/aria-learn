## What it is

Adding and taking away fractions that have the same denominator, such as three eighths add two eighths.

## The one idea

The denominator names the size of the piece. Adding eighths to eighths gives more eighths, so only the number of pieces changes.

## Common stumbles

- Adds the bottoms as well, and gets five sixteenths.
- Says five and forgets what size the pieces are.
- Takes away when asked to add.

## Two concrete models

- One fraction bar cut into eighths, with both groups of pieces shaded on it.
- A number line in eighths, jumping three and then two more.

## Worked example

Three eighths add two eighths. Three pieces of eight and two more pieces of eight make five pieces of eight. Five eighths.

## Language to use

- pieces of the same size
- how many eighths
- the bottom says the size

## Language to avoid

- common denominator
- numerator addition rule
- like terms
