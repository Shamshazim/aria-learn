## What it is

Adding two three-digit numbers with a total below a thousand, where a column can make ten or more and has to be traded into the next place.

## The one idea

Every place works the same way. Ten ones make a ten, and ten tens make a hundred.

## Common stumbles

- Writes each column total down whole, so one hundred fifty-eight add two hundred sixty-seven becomes three, eleven, fifteen.
- Never carries, and gets three hundred fifteen.
- Carries from the ones but forgets the hundred made by the tens, getting three hundred twenty-five.

## Two concrete models

- Place-value blocks: hundreds flats, tens rods and ones cubes, trading ten of any kind for one of the next.
- A written column sum with each carried one written small above the next column.

## Worked example

One hundred fifty-eight add two hundred sixty-seven. Eight and seven is fifteen: write five, carry one ten. Five tens, six tens and the carried ten is twelve tens: write two, carry one hundred. One hundred, two hundreds and the carried hundred is four hundreds. Four hundred twenty-five.

## Language to use

- ten ones make a ten
- ten tens make a hundred
- carry it to the next column

## Language to avoid

- regrouping across places
- the standard algorithm
- carry the surplus
