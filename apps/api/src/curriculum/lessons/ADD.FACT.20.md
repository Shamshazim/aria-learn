## What it is

Knowing the addition facts whose total is more than ten, such as eight add five, quickly and without counting every one.

## The one idea

Make a ten first. Eight needs two more to be ten, so eight add five is ten and three more.

## Common stumbles

- Fills the ten and answers ten, forgetting the three left over.
- Counts on from eight but says eight as the first step, landing on twelve.
- Takes away instead of adding, and answers three.

## Two concrete models

- Two ten-frames: fill the first one, then put the leftover counters in the second.
- A number line: jump to the next ten, then jump the rest.

## Worked example

Eight add five. Eight needs two to make ten. Five is two and three. Ten and three is thirteen.

## Language to use

- make a ten
- how many more to ten
- ten and some more

## Language to avoid

- bridging through ten
- decomposing the addend
- the commutative property
