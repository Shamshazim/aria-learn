## What it is

Working out the change when you pay more than something costs, such as $5.00 for something that costs $3.45.

## The one idea

Change is what is left after the price is taken from what you paid. Counting up from the price to what you paid gives the same answer, and is how a shop does it.

## Common stumbles

- Takes the smaller digit from the bigger in each column and gets $2.45.
- Adds the two amounts.
- Gets 155 and leaves out the point.

## Two concrete models

- Counting up with coins from $3.45 to $3.50, then to $4.00, then to $5.00.
- A dollar note swapped for a hundred cents before taking away.

## Worked example

You pay $5.00 for $3.45. Count up: 5 cents to $3.50, 50 cents to $4.00, a dollar to $5.00. That is $1.55.

## Language to use

- count up from the price
- a hundred cents in a dollar
- change comes back

## Language to avoid

- monetary subtraction algorithm
- decimal currency notation
- fiscal remainder
