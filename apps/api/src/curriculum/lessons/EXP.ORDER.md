## What it is

Working out an expression with more than one operation in the right order, such as 3 + 4 × 2.

## The one idea

Brackets first, then times and divide, then add and take away. Everyone agrees on this order, so an expression means the same to everyone who reads it.

## Common stumbles

- Works straight through from left to right, so 3 + 4 × 2 becomes 14.
- Ignores the brackets, so (3 + 4) × 2 becomes 11.
- Adds before multiplying, so 2 × 3 + 4 becomes 14.

## Two concrete models

- The expression with the part to do first circled, then rewritten with that part replaced by its answer.
- A shop story: two bags of three apples and four loose ones.

## Worked example

3 + 4 × 2. Times comes before add, so do 4 × 2 first: 8. Then 3 + 8 is 11.

## Language to use

- brackets first
- times and divide before add and take away
- circle the part to do first

## Language to avoid

- operator precedence
- BODMAS as a rule to recite
- associativity
