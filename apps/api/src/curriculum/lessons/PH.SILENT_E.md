## What it is

Reading words where a final e is not spoken but makes the vowel before it say its own name.

## The one idea

The e at the end is not a sound. It is a signal to the vowel in the middle.

## Common stumbles

- Reads the word as its short form, saying cap for cape and hop for hope.
- Pronounces the final e, giving cape a second syllable.
- Applies the rule to words that do not have it, changing bed into bede.

## Two concrete models

- Read the short word first, then add the e card and read it again, so the change is heard rather than explained.
- Word pairs side by side: cap and cape, hop and hope, kit and kite.

## Worked example

Read cap. Now add e. The a now says its name, so the word is cape. The e itself stays silent.

## Language to use

- the e is quiet
- the vowel says its name
- read it again

## Language to avoid

- magic e produces a long vowel digraph
- vowel-consonant-e syllable type
