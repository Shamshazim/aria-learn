## What it is

Deciding which of two two-digit numbers is greater, or whether they are equal, and writing the answer with <, > or =.

## The one idea

Compare the tens first; only when the tens are the same do the ones decide.

## Common stumbles

- Knows which number is bigger but points the sign the wrong way.
- Compares the ones digits, so 29 seems bigger than 31.
- Calls two numbers equal because their tens match, as with 42 and 47.

## Two concrete models

- Both numbers built from tens rods and ones cubes, the rods lined up side by side so the longer row of tens is easy to see.
- A number line with both numbers marked: the one further along is the greater.

## Worked example

Compare 29 and 31. Look at the tens: 29 has two tens, 31 has three tens. Three tens is more, so 31 is greater, and we write 29 < 31.

## Language to use

- greater than
- less than
- the same as
- compare the tens first

## Language to avoid

- inequality
- relational symbol
- magnitude
