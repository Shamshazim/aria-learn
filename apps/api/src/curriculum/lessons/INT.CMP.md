## What it is

Deciding which of two integers is bigger when one or both are negative, such as -7 and -3.

## The one idea

On a number line, further right is always bigger. Negative numbers sit to the left of zero, and the further left they go, the smaller they are.

## Common stumbles

- Compares the sizes and ignores the signs, so -7 looks bigger than -3.
- Knows which is bigger but points the sign the wrong way.
- Thinks zero is the smallest number there is.

## Two concrete models

- A number line from -10 to 10 with both numbers marked.
- A thermometer: minus seven degrees is colder than minus three.

## Worked example

Compare -7 and -3. On the number line, -3 is to the right of -7. Further right is bigger, so -7 < -3.

## Language to use

- further right is bigger
- below zero
- colder, owing more

## Language to avoid

- additive inverse
- the set of integers
- ordering relation
