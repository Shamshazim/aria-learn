## What it is

Taking one two-digit number from another when the ones on top are fewer than the ones below, so a ten has to be broken into ones.

## The one idea

One ten is ten ones. Breaking a ten changes how the number is built, not how big it is.

## Common stumbles

- Takes the smaller digit from the bigger in every column, so fifty-two take away eighteen becomes forty-six.
- Breaks a ten for the ones but leaves five tens in the tens column, getting forty-four.
- Decides two take away eight cannot be done and writes a zero, getting forty.

## Two concrete models

- Place-value blocks: build fifty-two, swap one ten rod for ten ones, then take away eighteen.
- A number line: jump back the ten first, then the eight, crossing a ten on the way.

## Worked example

Fifty-two take away eighteen. Two ones is not enough to take eight. Break a ten: now there are four tens and twelve ones. Twelve take away eight is four. Four tens take away one ten is three tens. Thirty-four.

## Language to use

- break a ten into ones
- what you have on top
- the tens column

## Language to avoid

- borrow (it is never given back)
- regroup the minuend
- you can't take a big number from a small one
