## What it is

Adding and taking away decimals to hundredths, such as 3.4 add 1.25, where one number has fewer places than the other.

## The one idea

Line up the points so tenths are added to tenths and hundredths to hundredths. Then it is the same column work as whole numbers.

## Common stumbles

- Lines up the right-hand digits instead of the points, and gets 1.59.
- Works the columns but leaves out the point, writing 465.
- Adds 3 and 1, then adds 4 and 25 as if they were whole numbers, getting 4.29.

## Two concrete models

- The numbers written one above the other with the points in one column, and a zero to fill the empty place.
- A hundred-square for the parts after the point, with forty and twenty-five shaded.

## Worked example

3.4 add 1.25. Line up the points and write 3.4 as 3.40. Hundredths: 0 and 5 is 5. Tenths: 4 and 2 is 6. Ones: 3 and 1 is 4. The point comes straight down. 4.65.

## Language to use

- line up the points
- give it a zero to fill the place
- tenths with tenths

## Language to avoid

- decimal alignment algorithm
- placeholder zero notation
- carrying the mantissa
