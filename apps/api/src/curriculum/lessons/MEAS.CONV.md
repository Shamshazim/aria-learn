## What it is

Changing an amount from a bigger unit to a smaller one, such as metres to centimetres.

## The one idea

A smaller unit fits more times, so the number gets bigger: multiply by how many small units make one big one. 1 m is 100 cm, so 3 m is 300 cm.

## Common stumbles

- Divides instead of multiplying, and gets 0.03.
- Multiplies by ten for every change of unit.
- Uses the wrong number from the table, such as 100 for kilometres or for an hour.

## Two concrete models

- A metre stick with its centimetres, laid down three times.
- A table of units with how many of each make the next.

## Worked example

How many cm in 3 m? One metre is 100 cm. Three metres is 3 times 100, which is 300 cm.

## Language to use

- smaller units, more of them
- how many make one
- times by the table number

## Language to avoid

- dimensional analysis
- conversion factor
- unit rate of exchange
